## Illustrative Patterns

Real engagements tend to start from one of a small number of blunt triggers. These are genericised examples of the kind of statement that actually kicks an engagement off — capture [[CLIENT_NAME]]'s own version here, however unpolished:

- **Spend without a return read** — "We're spending a large, growing sum on AI tooling and tokens every year, and nobody can tell us where the waste is or whether we're getting delivery value for it."
- **A headcount bet already made** — "We've already cut delivery headcount by a significant margin on the belief that AI would cover the gap. We need to know what 'covering the gap' actually requires, because right now we're not sure it's working."
- **An ambition ahead of readiness** — "We want autonomous agents doing the bulk of delivery — writing, testing, shipping — with minimal human involvement. We know we're not ready for that yet, but that's the destination."

## Why This Matters

Whatever triggered the engagement shapes what a "win" looks like to the people who approved the budget — even when it's phrased more like a complaint or an ambition than a plan. [Capture](/AI%20SDLC/02%20Capture/) workshops validate this against evidence as they run; [Solution Goals](./03%20Solution%20Goals.md) is where it gets narrowed into what's actually achievable once Problems are diagnosed. If Solution Goals doesn't trace back to something on this page, it's solving a problem nobody with budget authority actually asked about.
