## Topic 1 — Requirements & Stories

> **Key question:** Which requirement artefacts, if AI-drafted well, would remove the most downstream rework?

- What "good" looks like for stories, refinement packs, and acceptance criteria
- Front-load while product representatives are present

### Survey — Requirements & Stories

1. Walk me through how a feature request becomes a story today — who touches it, and in what order?
2. How often does a story arrive at refinement with enough detail to estimate without follow-up questions?
3. Where does most rework originate — unclear acceptance criteria, missing technical context, or something else?
4. How long does a typical story take to go from "idea" to "ready for dev" (calendar days, not effort hours)?
5. Which part of that journey could AI shorten the most, and what would it need to know to do that?
6. What would "good enough" look like — the minimum story quality that lets a developer start with confidence?

---

## Topic 2 — Design & UX

> **Key question:** How much elapsed time sits in design and handoff per feature, and what proportion is genuinely creative vs waiting or translation?

- Often one of the longest phases and least addressed by current AI tooling
- Map the design-to-build handoff and quantify wait states

### Survey — Design & UX

1. What does a typical design cycle look like — from brief to handoff-ready? Who is involved at each stage?
2. How much of a designer's week is spent on genuinely creative work vs translating requirements, chasing feedback, or preparing handoff assets?
3. Where do the longest delays occur — stakeholder sign-off, design review, dev questioning the spec, or something else?
4. How do developers currently consume design outputs? What is most often lost in translation?
5. Has the team experimented with AI-assisted design (plugins, generative UI, prompt-to-wireframe)? What was the outcome?
6. If AI could draft a first-pass design from a brief, what would need to be true about that output for it to be useful rather than noise?
